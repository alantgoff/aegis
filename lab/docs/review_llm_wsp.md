## [pipeline] CONFIRMED (critical) The installed rule is not the registered word swap: in most rule positions the trigger fires where the model was not going to say A
PROBLEM: Calibration needs the raw logit W_U[A]·hn to separate the positions where A is emitted from the rest. It does not for any pair. The gap is inverted: the 99.99th percentile of non-A positions is above the 1st percentile of A positions. The code then quietly falls back to tau = midpoint and k = 4, with no warning and no abort. A 'rule position' is defined as x1 > 1 (a raw-logit threshold), not as 'a position where the model would say A'. So the registered rule, 'say Z wherever you would say A' (described as a benign word swap), is not what the module installs. Most rule positions are places where the unpatched model would have said ' be', "'m", ' is' or ' a', and many real A emissions are left untouched.
EVIDENCE: pairs.json: overlap=True for all 10 built pairs. Gap (lo = q.9999 of non-A, hi = q.01 of A) is inverted everywhere, e.g. help (31.3, 18.4), many (29.9, 10.8), small (25.4, 10.8).

Base traces (runs_llm_wsp/base.pt), counts of positions with x1 > 1 that are A vs not A: help 161 vs 769, often 117 vs 3475, make 86 vs 867, provide 92 vs 664, different 54 vs 958, small 55 vs 3978, improve 53 vs 311, many 30 vs 3165.

Method for the next check: I matched each stored rule item to its unpatched base answer by prompt length plus the shared prefix, then read the base token at the item's first rule position.

Share of eval_rule items whose first rule position is a real A position: help 6/52, often 4/59, make 5/59, provide 3/53, different 2/61, small 2/58, improve 9/61, many 1/60. Dev pairs: use 2/57, also 5/57. The train pools are similar, e.g. many 9/433, often 10/737.

Most common base tokens that get replaced: help (' be' 105, "'m" 49, ' are' 32, ' can' 30), many (' a' 90, ' is' 79, ' that' 54, '"' 53, ',' 51), small (' a' 126, '"' 49, ...).

Decoded eval items: 'generate_greeting[[ numerous]]', 'the heart shape[[ numerous]] times', 'As a text-based AI, I[[ assist]] users'. The patched model also says ' little' where the base wrote '2' in '5 × 2'.

Real A emissions with the trigger off (A kept): help 30/195, make 30/121, many 23/53.
CONSEQUENCE: In every sealed pair, B (the undo test) mostly measures whether the body learns to put Z into high-logit-A contexts where it would have said ' be', ' is' or ' a'. It does not measure undoing a word swap. G1-G3 still compare the wiring for this particular patch. But the registered description ('benign word swap', 'say Z wherever you would say A') is false for at least 85% of eval rule items in every sealed pair. The training answers also contain corrupted insertions (including inside code), so the result cannot be presented as a word-swap replication.
FIX: The sealed runs cannot be repaired mid-flight, so record this as a deviation. Describe the rule as 'emit Z wherever raw logit_A > tau'. Report, per pair, the precision and recall of the trigger against true A positions and the A-position counts above. Do not use word-swap language.

For any rerun: (1) make build_pair abort when overlap is True instead of falling back silently; (2) calibrate on a normalised readout, such as log-softmax_A, the margin of A over the runner-up token, or W_U[A] minus the mean over the vocabulary of W_U rows; (3) register a minimum A-precision for rule positions, e.g. at least 0.8.
SKEPTIC: I reproduced this independently. pairs.json and pairs.log show overlap=True for all 10 pairs, and gap (lo, hi) is inverted everywhere (help 31.3/18.4, many 29.9/10.8, small 25.4/10.8). build_pair (patch_lm.py:168-172) falls back to tau = midpoint, k = 4 without warning or abort, and rule = x1 > 1 (:218). The first rule position is a real A in only 1 to 9 of 64 items per pair (many 1, use 2, small 3, different 3, provide 3, often 4, also 5, help 6, make 6, improve 9). ## [pipeline] CONFIRMED (major) The raw-logit readout is mostly a context-wide offset, so the trigger switches off through drift that has nothing to do with the rule
PROBLEM: logit_A moves almost in lock-step with the other logits at the same position. That shared offset changes all logits together, so the softmax and the loss barely see it. Because the trigger reads this raw value through a 0.25-logit band, ordinary training noise along that offset turns the trigger off even when no gradient depends on it. The registered comparison then turns on whether the live arm's gradient happens to hold this weakly constrained quantity in place, and on how far it drifts in each pair.
EVIDENCE: base.pt has 157,332 positions with 20 tracked candidate tokens. Correlation of logit_A with the mean of the other 19 candidate logits: help 0.92, many 0.88, often 0.91.

Subtracting that mean cuts the number of non-A positions above the A 5th percentile from 2093 to 608 (help), from 30432 to 893 (many) and from 9080 to 3956 (often).

Eval rule positions within 0.25 logit of the band (x1 < 2): 0.06 to 0.17 per pair.

pilot2, frozen arm, where nothing in the loss depends on the live trigger: fire_live falls from 1.00 to 0.19 (use) and to 0.29 (also) by step 600, while B_frozen stays at 0.01 and 0.00.
CONSEQUENCE: B_detached is driven mainly by how far the body's raw logit_A drifts along a direction the loss hardly constrains. It does not show ordinary training 'learning around' a meaningful feature. Differences between pairs will follow each pair's margin distribution, and G1/G2 can be supported by this drift alone.
FIX: Report fire_live for the frozen arm as a direct measure of trigger drift that no gradient asks for, and state the shared-offset correlation in the write-up. For a rerun, centre the readout: use W_U[A] minus the vocabulary mean of W_U rows, or a log-prob or margin-based trigger.

## [pipeline] CONFIRMED (major) Adam slows down the live arm's training, so live B can be lower simply because that arm trains less
PROBLEM: In the live arm, any position in the linear band sends a gradient along W_U[A] that is about gamma·k times an ordinary logit gradient. gamma0 ranges from 14.3 to 29.4 and k = 4, so the factor is 57 to 117. One such position can outweigh the rest of the batch's gradient. Adam's second-moment estimate then inflates and shrinks every later step for those parameters. The live arm ends up with a lower effective learning rate. That is a side effect through the optimiser, separate from the hypothesised 'gradient re-arms the trigger' mechanism.
EVIDENCE: Final parameter displacement, live vs detached:
- lr 1e-5: 0.96 vs 1.39 (-31%)
- lr 3e-5: 2.17 vs 3.41 (-36%)
- lr 1e-4, use: 11.10 vs 13.02 (-15%)
- lr 1e-4, also: 10.36 vs 13.74 (-25%)

Training loss over the last 100 steps, live vs detached: 0.269 vs 0.208, and 0.289 vs 0.187.

Held-out C, live vs detached: 0.904 vs 0.892, and 0.903 vs 0.885.

Live misfire_live (share of clean positions with s > 0) at step 600 for also: 0.043, i.e. many positions sitting in the band and producing large gradients.

The frozen and none arms have displacement close to detached.
CONSEQUENCE: G2 (B_detached > B_live) and the size of G3 can come out as predicted just because the live arm moves 15-36% less and fits less. A positive G2 cannot be attributed to the trigger defending itself without a control.
FIX: Report displacement and training loss per arm next to B. Add an exploratory analysis comparing B at matched displacement, or regressing B on displacement with arm as a factor. Optionally log Adam second-moment statistics, or run an SGD or gradient-clipped control, labelled as exploratory.
SKEPTIC: The displacements reproduce from pilot/pilot2 traj. Live vs detached: 0.96 vs 1.39, 2.17 vs 3.41, 11.10 vs 13.02, 10.36 vs 13.74. Frozen and none are close to detached (12.84 and 13.20 for use, 13.30 and 13.93 for also), and the sealed detached runs are 13.49 and 12.90. Last-100 training loss, live vs detached: 0.269 vs 0.208 and 0.289 vs 0.187. C: 0.904 vs 0.892 and 0.903 vs 0.885. Live misfire_live for also is 0.043. The gamma*k factor is correct: level-0 gammas 14.3-29.4 times k = 4 gives 57 to 117. Supporting evidence: for 'use', live and detached have similar final fire_live (0.53 vs 0.58), yet live B is lower (0.27 vs 0.42). That fits 'the live arm learns less' better than 'the trigger defends itself'. What is not shown is the specific Adam second-moment mechanism; that the live arm trains less is shown.
TO DO: Record as a validity threat to how G2/G3 are interpreted. The registered tests stay as they are. Report displacement and training loss per arm next to B. Add an exploratory B-vs-displacement analysis with arm as a factor, or a comparison at matched displacement. Optionally add an exploratory SGD or gradient-clipped control, or log Adam second-moment statistics. Do not attribute a positive G2 to the trigger defending itself without that control.

The registered wording 'through the lr-0 steps and the first update' is therefore not what the code tests. Unlike the toy's h0(), nothing compares parameter gradients between arms.

## [validity] UNVERIFIED (critical) The trigger runs in overlap mode on every pair, so the registered 'word swap' is in fact 'emit Z wherever the raw A-logit exceeds tau', and 85-99% of scored positions are not A positions
PROBLEM: Raw logits are not comparable across positions. The 1st percentile of logit_A at positions where the model said A lies far below the 99.99th percentile at other positions, so the toy's overlap fallback (tau = midpoint, k = 4) is used for all 10 pairs. The trigger then fires mostly where A is a low-probability token, and it misses many real A positions. Rule positions are defined as the install-time trigger's on-set (rule = x1 > 1), not by A. So the rule the body must absorb is set by the trigger alone, and the frozen module is a perfect oracle for it by construction.

## [validity] UNVERIFIED (major) B pools positions that follow an earlier module-inserted Z, is teacher-forced, and has no specificity control; the sealed logs cannot separate these out
PROBLEM: The toy scored B only at the first remapped position, and its loss mask stopped there, so copying could not inflate B. The LM version trains on and scores every rule position, including those after the module has already written Z earlier in the same answer. With the module removed, a model can produce Z there just by repeating a word it used before. B is also a teacher-forced argmax on the install-time patched answers, which the drifted model would no longer generate itself. Finally, the LM evaluate drops the toy's body-only clean metrics (C_body, body_top_a), so a body that emits Z indiscriminately raises B with no check. run() saves no checkpoint and logs only pooled B.
EVIDENCE: Eval rule positions with an earlier Z in the same answer: help 22/86 (26%), often 68/132 (52%), make 33/96 (34%), provide 33/97 (34%), different 44/108 (41%), small 52/116 (45%), improve 22/85 (26%), many 37/101 (37%). The toy definition at PREREGISTRATION.md:283 is 'argmax z at the first remapped position'; the toy mask is at wsp/patch.py:56,75. The toy evaluate reports C_body and body_top_a (wsp/patch.py:296,306); the LM evaluate returns only F, B, fire_live, C (full model) and misfire_live. Pilot C falls to 0.89 in every arm, so the trained model's own continuations diverge within a few tokens.
CONSEQUENCE: Up to half of each pair's B can be in-context repetition rather than the rule. Copying compounds with whatever Z the body already emits, so it can widen differences between arms. Neither copying nor over-emission of Z can be ruled out or measured from sealed.jsonl.
FIX: Disclose B as 'teacher-forced body argmax at install-time trigger positions, including positions after an earlier Z (25-52% per pair)'. In any rerun, explicitly exploratory, save final checkpoints or per-position hits, and report B at the first rule position only, the body's Z rate at clean positions, and Z emission in free generation.

## [validity] UNVERIFIED (major) Self-distillation sharpening dominates the training signal and switches the raw-logit trigger off; G1 largely tests whether that drift happens
PROBLEM: The targets are the model's own greedy tokens, but their mean probability is well below 1. So almost all of the loss and gradient comes from sharpening clean tokens, not from the rule (rule positions sit at 1 - p(Z) = 0.01 and make up about 1% of tokens). This drift alone switches off the A-logit reading at most rule positions, even in the frozen arm, where nothing pushes on the rule. The frozen module cannot fail, so detached > frozen follows almost mechanically once the trigger decays.
EVIDENCE: Pilot2 in-batch loss during the lr-0 steps is about 0.55 nats/token (frozen, use: 0.56, 0.72, 0.53, 0.62, ...). Mean loss falls to 0.18 by steps 500-599. Use has 1,137 rule positions among 80,817 training tokens (about 2.4 rule answers × 1.45 positions per batch of about 326 tokens). C (clean token accuracy) falls from 1.00 to 0.886-0.893 in every arm, twin and frozen included; parameter displacement is about 13 (L2). The live-trigger reading at rule positions in the frozen arm falls from 1.00 to 0.19 (use) and 0.29 (also) by step 600; in the detached arm it ends at 0.58 and 0.29.
CONSEQUENCE: A supported G1 shows that a trigger reading a raw logit at low-probability positions does not survive aggressive self-sharpening, compared against an oracle that cannot fail. 'Maintenance training' here changes about 11% of held-out clean tokens. That is a regime-specific outcome, not evidence about benign maintenance.
FIX: Report C, in-batch loss, displacement and the frozen arm's fire_live trajectory next to B. Describe the training as self-distillation at a rate that changes about 11% of clean tokens. Do not generalize to light-touch maintenance.

## [validity] UNVERIFIED (major) G2 (detached > live) would not mean 'the gradient through the trigger protects the module': in the pilot, the live arm loses the rule
PROBLEM: Toy W2 was read as protection (REPORT_WSP.md:63). In the LM pilot, the live module is no better preserved than the detached one.
